⚓ Strait of Hormuz: Iran Missile Attack on ADNOC Tanker and Escalating Strait Crisis

Key summary
  • The United Arab Emirates (UAE) announced that a tanker belonging to its state oil company ADNOC was hit by an Iranian missile attack while transiting the Strait of Hormuz; no casualties were confirmed, and Qatar immediately issued a statement condemning the attack. Iranian media reported that a fire broke out on the vessel in the southern part of the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) reaffirmed that the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz has nothing to do with negotiations with Oman and that the United States must accept Iran's conditions (including compensation for violations of the MOU); the judiciary also warned that it 'will surely respond to the enemy's crimes.' However, Iran's Foreign Minister stated that Iran is 'very close' to an agreement with Oman on a temporary transit route through Hormuz, raising the possibility of normalized passage at the same time.
  • A trilateral defense agreement was also signed in Mecca, reflecting a broader realignment of regional security arrangements; Ali Akbar Velayati, an adviser to Iran's Supreme Leader, argued that 'foreign forces must withdraw from the region and regional states can guarantee their own security.'
  • Domestically, reports emerged that crude oil loadings at Iran's Kharg Island have been halted for at least a week due to a US naval blockade; that top US military leadership (the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff) is seeking an exit strategy from the war with Iran due to munitions depletion and the limits of airstrikes; and that China has made direct contact with the Houthis requesting that its own vessels be excluded from Red Sea and Hormuz logistics risks.
